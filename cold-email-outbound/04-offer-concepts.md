# Offer Concepts

The free Opinion of Value (OOV) is the industry-standard offer for business brokers. It works. It's also what every other broker is offering, which means it's not a competitive moat. The opportunity isn't to abandon the OOV — it's to build a small portfolio of alternative offers that map to where prospects actually are in their exit thinking, and A/B test them against the OOV baseline.

## Mental model: where the prospect is in exit thinking

Most owners cycle through three phases on the way to a sale:

| Phase | Mindset | Best offers |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| **Curious** | "Could I sell? Is it even an option?" | Industry exit guide, comparable sales report |
| **Researching** | "What would I actually get for this business?" | OOV, exit readiness assessment |
| **Deciding** | "Am I ready to do this — and with whom?" | Buyer match, 15-minute exit strategy call |

A single offer can't optimally serve all three. A *portfolio* with the OOV as control can.

## The portfolio (offers to test)

### 1. Free Opinion of Value (control / baseline)

The status quo. Personalized valuation estimate based on light financial data and industry comparables. Strong for the **researching** phase. Industry default — every broker offers this.

CTA pattern: "Would a complimentary opinion of value on [business] be useful?"

### 2. Comparable Sales Report

A report covering the last 5–10 businesses similar to theirs that sold in their geography or industry, with sale prices, multiples, and transaction context. Sells research, not a relationship. Lower commitment than the OOV. Best for the **curious** phase.

CTA pattern: "I just pulled the last 8 [industry] businesses that sold in [region] — happy to send the report if it's useful."

Why it can outperform OOV: the prospect doesn't have to share anything about their own business to receive value. Reciprocity kicks in once they've consumed the report.

### 3. Exit Readiness Assessment

A structured scorecard grading the business across 8–10 dimensions (financial cleanup, customer concentration, owner dependency, processes documented, key-person risk, recurring revenue mix, etc.) with a numeric readiness score. Sells *preparation*, not *transaction*. Best for owners who suspect they're not ready.

CTA pattern: "I help owners think about how 'sale-ready' their business is before they go to market — open to a quick check?"

Why it can outperform OOV: it positions you as a strategic advisor, not a salesperson. It also flushes out the owners who *aren't* sale-ready — which is high-value qualification data.

### 4. Industry-Specific Exit Guide (downloadable)

A PDF specific to their vertical: "Selling Your HVAC Business in 2026" or "What Multi-Unit Franchisees Need to Know Before Exiting." Market data, common pitfalls, valuation drivers. Pure content offer. Lowest friction. Best as a **top-of-funnel magnet** rather than direct outbound CTA — it builds the list and authority but rarely converts directly to a meeting.

CTA pattern: "Wrote a short guide on [topic] specific to [vertical] — want me to send it?"

Why it can outperform OOV: it's the only offer that takes zero owner commitment. Every owner can say yes to "send me a guide."

### 5. Buyer Match

"Based on what you've built, I'd like to know if there are buyers I'm currently working with who'd be interested in a business like yours." Implies *demand exists for their specific business*, which flips the dynamic — the seller is no longer prospecting buyers, the buyer is prospecting them.

CTA pattern: "We're tracking active buyers in [vertical] right now. If you'd ever consider an offer, would it be worth a quick conversation?"

Why it can outperform OOV: it makes the prospect feel sought-after rather than solicited. *Requires you to actually have active buyers* — don't run this offer if you can't credibly back it up.

### 6. 15-Minute Exit Strategy Call

Same outcome as the OOV (a call) but framed around *strategy*, not *valuation*. Some owners feel "valuation" is too transactional but will take "strategy" all day.

CTA pattern: "Worth a 15-minute conversation about how owners in [vertical] are thinking about exits in 2026?"

Why it can outperform OOV: pure framing. Same call, lower-stakes language.

### 7. Succession Planning Conversation

Broader than just sale — includes family transitions, partner buyouts, ESOPs, gradual handoffs. Maps to owners who haven't decided "sell vs. transition" yet. Wider top of funnel.

CTA pattern: "Helping owners think through succession — whether that's selling, transitioning to family, or stepping back gradually. Open to a brief conversation?"

Why it can outperform OOV: serves a broader audience. Some of those who pick "succession" eventually convert to sellers anyway.

### 8. Paid Premium Valuation (future — pending new valuation software)

A deeper, paid valuation product using the new valuation software currently being built with an outside partner (a few weeks from launch as of May 2026). Distinct from the free OOV — designed for owners who want a more rigorous, defensible valuation without committing to a sale-side engagement, or owners who aren't ready to sell but want a clear-eyed read on where their business stands.

**CTA pattern (once the product exists):** "We just rolled out a deeper valuation product — same insights you'd get pre-listing, but standalone and not tied to selling. Worth $X to know exactly where you stand?"

**Why it can complement (not replace) the OOV:**

- The free OOV serves the "researching" phase prospect who's open to selling
- The paid valuation serves a slightly different mindset — owners who want clarity without a relationship, or who want a defensible third-party number for tax planning, partner buyouts, estate work, or insurance reasons
- It's also a revenue line in itself, not just a lead magnet
- Paid prospects who get a valuation and then decide to sell self-select as serious sellers — likely higher Toolkit/Full Service conversion than free OOV recipients

**Test plan once the software is live:**

1. First — does the paid valuation generate any cold reply at all? (the offer is fundamentally different — paying for what others give away free)
2. If yes — what audience converts on it? (early hypothesis: pre-exit-thinking owners doing tax/estate planning, partners contemplating buyouts, owners with 3–5 year horizons rather than 6–12 month)
3. Compare downstream signed-agreement rates: free-OOV-then-signs vs. paid-valuation-then-signs

**Status:** Not testable yet. Slot into the test order after current alternatives (Comparable Sales Report, Exit Readiness Assessment, Buyer Match) when the software ships.

## How to test the portfolio

Don't replace the OOV. Run it as the control variant.

For each new sequence in Phase 1+, randomly assign each prospect to one offer variant out of (a) OOV control, (b) one alternative being tested. Keep everything else identical between the two — same subject line family, same opening signal pattern, same length, same sender. The only difference is the CTA.

Track:
- **Reply rate** (any reply)
- **Positive reply rate** (replies that engage rather than unsubscribe)
- **Booked-call rate** (out of positive replies)
- **OOV-or-equivalent-delivered rate** (i.e., did the offer actually get delivered after they said yes?)
- **Listing conversion rate** (did the conversation lead to a representation agreement, eventually?)

Volume requirement for statistically meaningful results: at least 500 records per variant before drawing conclusions. At 10K/month volume, that's a week or less per test.

## Suggested test order

1. **OOV vs. Comparable Sales Report** — first test, broadest applicability, reveals whether "less commitment, more research" outperforms "personal valuation"
2. **OOV vs. Exit Readiness Assessment** — second test, reveals whether the prep-vs-transaction framing converts better
3. **OOV vs. Buyer Match** — third test, reveals whether the demand-flip framing converts (only run if you have credible active buyers)
4. **OOV vs. Paid Premium Valuation** — fourth test, run only after the new valuation software ships. Reveals whether a paid offer can pull a different prospect type into the funnel

Run one test at a time. Multiple simultaneous tests muddy the results unless we have very high volume.

## What we don't know yet (and how to find out)

- Which offer converts best per persona (franchisee vs. independent owner). Don't assume the same offer wins on both brands; test separately.
- Whether geography matters for offer choice. Possibly — owners in highly active M&A markets may respond differently to "Buyer Match" than owners in quiet markets.
- Whether owner age changes optimal offer. Hypothesis: 55–65 → "researching" offers; 65+ → "deciding" offers; 45–55 → "curious" offers. Test in time.
